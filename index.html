Three steps from search to contact

The whole journey is built so that someone under time pressure reaches a usable answer in as few steps as possible.

Narrow to your area

Pick district, upazila and local area, then a blood group. Filtering by location first removes results that are too far away to help today.

Check who is available

Every donor carries an availability status derived from their last donation date, so nobody wastes calls on a donor inside the cooldown period.

Contact, or post a request

Open a profile to reach the donor. If no one nearby matches, post an emergency request and check hospital blood-bank stock in the same place.

What the platform keeps visible

Location, availability and urgency drive every screen. These are the fields the system surfaces so a decision can be made quickly.

  • Blood group and the units required
  • Last donation date and next possible availability
  • Available or unavailable, never left ambiguous
  • Hospital blood-bank units by group
  • The date each stock record was last verified
  • Urgent and upcoming requirements on one board

How urgency is read

The label sets where a request sits on the board and how prominently it is shown.

CRITICAL
Needed within roughly 24 hours. Shown first, with the alert colour.
URGENT
Needed within a few days. Highlighted, but below critical requests.
PLANNED
A scheduled requirement, such as surgery, posted early to gather donors.

This prototype has no backend. Submitted requests live in the browser session and disappear on reload.

Your availability, calculated

BloodNet marks a donor unavailable for three months after a recorded donation and shows the next possible date.

The three-month cooldown is proposed project logic, not medical clearance. Final donor eligibility must be confirmed by qualified healthcare professionals.
